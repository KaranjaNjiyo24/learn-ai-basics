Cane Yield Planner

Draft — for your review

A phone-friendly app that gives a sugarcane farmer an honest pre-harvest estimate of what his land will produce, so he can plan money and labor.

How it fits together

The unique kernel

The farmer enters what he already knows about his own land, and the app uses his phone's location to pull local weather itself. He gets a yield estimate for his plot, shown as a low-to-high range, not a generic average. It is honest about being a reference-based estimate until real harvest data exists to learn from.

Who it's for

John, a sugarcane farmer who leased 10 acres. The seed variety comes from the sugar company he sells to; he knows his soil composition and location. Today he has "no idea how much I will harvest," so he can't plan money and labor with confidence.

The core loop & what "working" looks like

He opens the app, enters acres, soil values and cane variety, and allows location. Weather appears, and he sees an expected harvest for his land with a range and a planning note. He comes back through the season, and later to compare against his real harvest.

Proof-of-concept boundary

Now — in the proof of concept
  • One user (John), one crop (sugarcane)
  • Inputs: acres, soil composition, cane variety, location from phone GPS
  • Weather fetched automatically for that location
  • Reference-based yield estimate adjusted by those inputs, shown as a range and labeled as an estimate
  • A short plain-language planning note for money and labor
Later
  • Train a real model on actual harvest data so it improves over time
  • Cost per acre from market information, and profit planning
  • Leasing companies and early-career farmers as users
  • Other crops and regions
Explicitly cut
  • Claiming prediction accuracy — no yield data yet to support it
  • Market price / cost-per-acre — not needed for the harvest-expectation decision; revisit once yield works
  • Multiple user types and crops — one specific user keeps it testable
