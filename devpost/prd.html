PRD draft — for your review

A phone-friendly app that gives a sugarcane farmer an honest pre-harvest estimate of yield per acre, so he can plan money and labor. Built first for John, with 10 leased acres.

The core journey

From scope to specifics

Scope said

"He enters what he knows about his land, and the app uses his phone's location to pull the weather."

Now we know

Eight typed or picked fields (acres, pH, nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, variety, planting date, location), a manual location fallback, a weather retry, and a "typical conditions" label.

Screens, behavior, and checks

A sugarcane stalk grows to full height in cane green, then a "Did you know?" pop-up shows one fact from a short list you verify against a source you trust. Facts are never generated by the app.

  • The animation plays and ends on the pop-up.
  • The pop-up shows one verified fact and can be closed.
  • Closing it leads to the input screen.

One column, built for a phone. Typed: acres, pH, nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, cane variety. Date picker: planting date.

  • Every field is present and works with a phone keyboard or date picker.
If it goes wrong
  • Blank field: a gentle nudge to fill it in.
  • Unlikely number (pH 40, negative acres): "That looks unusual — are you sure?" to confirm or correct.
  • First use: empty form with short helper text; no result until he runs it.

The app asks to use the phone's location and fetches rainfall and temperature for that spot.

  • Allowing location fills it in and the weather appears.
  • Refusing lets him type his location and continue.
If it goes wrong
  • Weather can't be fetched: a plain message and "try again". He can continue, and the result is marked "based on typical conditions, not live weather".

Expected tonnes per acre as a low-to-high range, the total for his acres, and a short planning note for money and labor. Always labeled an estimate.

  • Valid inputs give a range per acre and a total for his acres.
  • The result is labeled as an estimate.
  • More acres changes only the total.
  • A planning note appears and refers to the numbers shown.

Optional: new-season price per tonne, last season's price per tonne, last year's spending. Built last, first to drop if time runs short.

  • Leaving it blank does not affect the yield estimate.
  • A new price gives an expected income range.
  • Last season's price gives a comparison.

Look and feel

Brown #964B00 · Green #008000 · White #FFFFFF

The app (not this review page) uses only these flat colors, with no tints, shades, or gradients. Brown for text, borders, and buttons; green for the cane animation and main action. Square corners. A prominent serif (Times New Roman style). Modest button sizes. Plain and warm. Codes confirmed.

Build order and boundary

What we're building (in order)
  1. Input screen with friendly checks
  2. Location (manual fallback) and weather, with its failure state
  3. Yield estimate, result screen, planning note
  4. Opening animation and verified-fact pop-up
  5. Optional money section
Deferred from the POC
  • Reading a soil report from a photo (OCR)
  • Saving results, accounts, logins
  • Saving actual post-harvest prices
Non-goals
  • Claiming prediction accuracy
  • Market price tracking
  • Multiple crops or user types
